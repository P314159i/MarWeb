Vaginitis Educational Reference

Condition overviews, pathophysiology, and treatment summaries

Clinical Reference
🦠

Bacterial Vaginosis (BV)

Polymicrobial dysbiosis of vaginal flora

Overview

  • Replacement of normal Lactobacillus flora with anaerobes (Gardnerella, Prevotella, Mobiluncus)
  • Most common vaginal condition in women of reproductive age
  • Not a classic STI, but sexual activity is a risk factor
  • Can be asymptomatic; always treat in pregnancy

Key Symptoms

  • Malodorous fishy vaginal odor (worse after intercourse)
  • Thin, homogeneous gray-white discharge
  • Minimal or no itching (distinguishes from candidiasis)
  • Vaginal pH > 4.5

Amsel Criteria (≥3 of 4 = BV)

  • Thin, homogeneous vaginal discharge
  • Vaginal pH > 4.5
  • Positive whiff test (amine odor with KOH)
  • Clue cells >20% on wet mount

First-Line Treatment

  • Metronidazole 500 mg PO BID × 7 days
  • Metronidazole 0.75% gel intravaginally × 5 days
  • Clindamycin 2% cream intravaginally × 7 days
  • Tinidazole 1000 mg PO daily × 5 days (alternative)
🍄

Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (VVC)

Candida overgrowth of vaginal epithelium

Overview

  • Caused primarily by Candida albicans; non-albicans in recurrent/resistant cases
  • Disruption of normal flora allows Candida overgrowth
  • Classified as uncomplicated vs complicated (recurrent, severe, non-albicans, immunocompromised)

Key Symptoms

  • Intense vaginal and vulvar itching (hallmark)
  • Thick, white, cottage-cheese-like discharge
  • Vaginal burning, dyspareunia, dysuria
  • pH typically ≤ 4.5 (normal)

Risk Factors

  • Recent/prolonged antibiotic use
  • Diabetes mellitus (elevated glucose feeds Candida)
  • Immunocompromised state (HIV, chemotherapy, steroids)
  • Pregnancy, high-dose estrogen OCP use

First-Line Treatment

  • Fluconazole 150 mg PO single dose (uncomplicated)
  • Clotrimazole or Miconazole cream intravaginally × 7 days
  • Recurrent: Fluconazole 150 mg q72h × 3 doses, then weekly × 6 months
🦠

Trichomoniasis

Sexually transmitted protozoal infection

Overview

  • Caused by Trichomonas vaginalis, a flagellated protozoan — most common curable STI worldwide
  • Can be asymptomatic, especially in men
  • Increases susceptibility to HIV and other STIs

Key Symptoms

  • Copious, frothy, yellow-green vaginal discharge
  • Vaginal pH > 4.5
  • "Strawberry cervix" — pathognomonic
  • Dysuria, dyspareunia, pruritus, postcoital bleeding

Diagnosis

  • Motile trichomonads on wet mount (gold standard)
  • NAAT testing (highest sensitivity)
  • Screen for concurrent STIs (gonorrhea, chlamydia, HIV)

Treatment

  • Metronidazole 500 mg PO BID × 7 days (preferred for women)
  • Treat sexual partners simultaneously — this is an STI
  • Test-of-cure at 3 months
🌿

Atrophic Vaginitis / GSM

Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause

Overview

  • Caused by estrogen deficiency in postmenopausal women — not an infection
  • Chronic and progressive without treatment; affects ~50% of postmenopausal women

Key Symptoms

  • Dyspareunia (primary complaint), vaginal dryness and burning
  • Urinary frequency, urgency, incontinence, recurrent UTIs
  • Pale, thin, dry vaginal walls on exam; pH often > 5.0

Treatment — Local Estrogen (First-Line)

  • Estradiol vaginal tablet 10 mcg: nightly × 2 weeks, then 2×/week
  • Estriol cream 0.5 mg/g: nightly × 2 weeks, then 2×/week
  • Apply around urethra too if urinary symptoms present

Non-Hormonal Options

  • Water-based vaginal lubricants (for intercourse)
  • Vaginal moisturizers with hyaluronic acid (regular use)
  • Ospemifene 60 mg PO daily (SERM — oral option)
